5 Gentle Movement Breaks for Desk Days

Five simple seated movements to discuss with your teacher when you want a break from sitting.

Set up a comfortable seat

These ideas are adapted from the NHS sitting-exercise guidance. Choose a stable chair without wheels, keep your feet supported, and make room around you. If you are unsure whether a movement is suitable, check with a health professional involved in your care. Stop if anything feels painful.

1. Open the chest

Sit tall, ease your shoulders back and down, and gently open your arms to the sides. Keep the movement comfortable and avoid forcing your back into an arch.

2. Turn the upper body

With your feet planted, slowly turn your upper body a little to one side. Return to the middle before exploring the other side. Stay within an easy range.

3. Lift one knee

Hold the chair for support and lift one bent knee slightly, then lower it with control. Alternate sides without rushing.

4. Move the ankles

Extend one leg as comfortable and gently point your toes away, then bring them back towards you. Repeat on the other side.

5. Turn the head gently

With your shoulders relaxed, slowly look to one side and return to centre. Try the other side without pulling or pressing on your head. These are movement breaks, not a treatment for persistent pain; get individual advice if symptoms concern you.

Further reading: NHS: Sitting exercises. This general guide is not individual medical advice.
