Breathwork · 30 Oct 2026

Making Time for Breathing and Stillness

What to expect from a gentle introduction to breath awareness, without pressure to force or hold your breath.

A pause within the practice

Yoga can include movement, breathing and meditation. A quieter part of a class offers time to settle into a comfortable position and notice how you feel. It does not need to be an elaborate exercise or a performance.

Start with observation

A beginner session can start by noticing your natural breathing, rather than trying to make it deeper, longer or more impressive. Ask the teacher to explain the purpose of a technique before trying it. You can keep your eyes open and choose a supported seat.

Comfort is useful feedback

Let the teacher know if a technique feels uncomfortable, makes you light-headed, or brings up concerns. Return to your normal breathing and pause if needed. Forceful breathing and long breath holds are not a necessary starting point for a beginner.

Make room for individual guidance

Tell the teacher about relevant concerns before a session, and get individual professional advice if you are unsure what is appropriate for you. Claims about breathing practices vary, and a general studio class is not a substitute for medical care. A useful first goal is simply to understand the instructions and find a comfortable way to participate.

Further reading: NCCIH: Yoga effectiveness and safety. This general guide is not individual medical advice.
